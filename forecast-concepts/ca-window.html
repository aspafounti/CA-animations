Likely Forecast Based on current indicators

If Israel invades Lebanon, several indicators suggest that Iran will take significant actions. Multiple sources indicate that Iran has explicitly warned of direct intervention if Israel attacks Lebanon.1 2 3 4 5 This is reinforced by statements from Iranian officials and affiliated groups.

Iran's potential actions could include direct military intervention or support for its proxies. Hezbollah is expected to escalate its aggression against Israel in response to an invasion.10 11 12 13 14 This is consistent with Iran's strategy of using proxy forces to exert influence.

Additionally, Iran has reportedly prepared for a broader regional conflict, indicating readiness to engage in a multi-front war involving its allies in Syria, Iraq, and Yemen.15 18 19 20

The likelihood of indirect action is almost certain, while direct action is a realistic possibility to likely, depending on the evolution of the conflict and the crossing of Iranian red lines.

Hatch on response background

The scrollable text area gets the hatch — same vocabulary as the card, applied to the prose surface. A small LIKELY + Forecast label sits at the very top of the text, anchoring the treatment.

The hatch is at lower opacity than the card (~3% stroke alpha vs ~6%) so body text stays fully readable. The treatment is persistent — it doesn't scroll away.

Works well when: the entire response is a forecast. Clearly different from a standard response at a glance.

Watch out for: long responses where the hatch becomes fatiguing. Could reduce opacity further on scroll depth.

If Israel invades Lebanon, what actions will Iran take and how likely is direct military intervention?
‹ 1/3 ›
| Quick Answers | Default

If Israel invades Lebanon, multiple sources indicate Iran has explicitly warned of direct intervention.1 2 3 4 5 Statements from Iranian officials and affiliated groups reinforce this, describing an Israeli invasion as a red line requiring a response.6 7 8 9

Likely Projected
Iran's most probable response is proxy escalation via Hezbollah, with a likely (65–75%) probability of sustained cross-border operations from Lebanon within 48–72 hours of any ground incursion. Direct Iranian military action carries a realistic possibility (35–50%), contingent on the scale of Israeli advance and whether declared red lines are crossed.

Iran has reportedly prepared for a broader regional conflict, with readiness indicators from Syria, Iraq, and Yemen-based allied forces.15 18 19 20 The U.S. has separately warned Israel that an invasion could compel Iranian intervention — underscoring the international dimension.2 5 8 9

Possible Contingent
If Israeli ground forces advance beyond the Litani River, the probability of direct Iranian missile or drone strikes on Israeli territory increases to likely (60–70%). This threshold has been referenced in IRGC communications intercepted in the past 30 days.11 12

Primary indicators to monitor: proxy mobilisation rates, IRGC advisory presence shifts, and official Iranian escalatory rhetoric crossing from conditional to unconditional framing.

@📎 Default

Inline paragraph callout

Only the probability-bearing passages get the hatch treatment — lifted into a styled callout block. The surrounding confirmed/analytical prose stays clean.

This is the most granular approach and the most honest — it distinguishes "here is what happened" from "here is what we project" at the sentence level rather than labelling the whole response.

Works well when: CA mixes confirmed analysis with forecasts in a single response. Lets the analyst immediately identify which claims are projections without re-reading.

Watch out for: requires CA to identify which sentences are forecast-specific. That's an inference task — ensure the model can reliably tag its own probabilistic statements.
