Forecasting — Visual Treatment Concepts

Five approaches for distinguishing predicted/projected content from confirmed events across CA surfaces.

What makes it work: The dashed stroke is already the global convention for "projected" — financial charts, weather maps, engineering drawings all use it. No new vocabulary to learn. The treatment layers cleanly on top of any colour scheme since it modifies stroke style, not hue. On the shortcut card it manifests as a dashed border; on the graph it continues the confirmed line as a dashed extension; on cards and stream panels it replaces the solid border.

What makes it work: The glow is additive — it sits on top of whatever colour the widget already uses. A red widget stays red; a yellow widget stays yellow. The glow and reduced opacity are the consistent signal, not the hue. The pulsing dot reinforces "live projection" without being noisy. On dark backgrounds the glow reads immediately as something speculative and alive, distinct from the flat solidity of confirmed data.

What makes it work: Hatching is the cartographic and engineering convention for "this area is different / uncertain." It overlays on top of any colour without changing the hue — the red widget stays red, but you immediately know it's a projection. At small sizes the pattern is subtle; on larger surfaces (map zone overlays, graph area fills) it becomes more prominent. The texture density could also encode confidence: sparse hatching = high confidence, dense hatching = speculative.

What makes it work: The fade direction is always semantic — it points toward "forward." On a timeline card the right edge dissolves; on a map a forecast zone fades outward from its centre; on a graph the projection trail loses opacity. This is the most cinematic of the five concepts and works especially well on stream cards and larger panels. The "Projection" tag provides an explicit label for contexts where the fade alone might be too subtle (small widgets).

What makes it work: The wave mark is colour-matched to whatever widget it lives in — yellow widget gets a yellow wave, red widget gets a red wave. The mark itself is the constant; the colour is inherited. This makes it the most robust solution for the multi-colour widget system. It's also the most brandable: like a lock icon or a live badge, it becomes an immediately learnable glyph. The mark can animate (slow continuous scroll) for active live forecasts vs static for historical projections.
